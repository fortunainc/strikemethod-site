# Options qualified interview and price test

Status: ready for authorized recruitment; no outreach sent.

## Recruit profile

Include only participants who traded options in the last 30 days and meet at least one of: currently pay for a research/journal/alert tool; canceled one in the last six months; can show a recent workflow with conflicting inputs; routinely decides among multiple setups. Exclude friends/founder relationships from commercial gate counts and record incentives separately.

## Neutral screener

1. When did you last open or close an options position?
2. Which tools did you use for that decision? Which are paid, and what is the current monthly total?
3. Have you canceled or switched an options tool in the last six months? What happened?
4. Are your typical positions intraday, multi-day swing, or longer-term?
5. May we discuss one actual recent decision and, if comfortable, view the non-sensitive parts of your workflow?

## Interview guide

1. Walk me through the last options decision from first idea through exit or rejection. Do not summarize—show the sequence.
2. What did you check, in order? Where did signals disagree?
3. What work was still left after your tools produced their outputs?
4. What made you act, wait, or pass? What was defined before entry: contract, entry, size/risk, invalidation, targets, and exit?
5. What do you record permanently? When do you revisit it? Show the last time that record changed a later decision.
6. Which paid tool would be removed if another product reliably delivered a short list of complete, checkable decisions? Why might you keep both?
7. Some days TSM may say nothing qualifies. Describe the last day you should have stood down. Would a quiet product feel protective or broken? What evidence would make you trust the silence?
8. Review a real, correctly labeled TSM record only if production truth permits. Ask the participant to explain what they believe it promises; correct misunderstandings.
9. What would stop you from relying on this: missing evidence, transparent losses, too few decisions, delayed timing, contract fit, trust, or something else?

## Price tradeoff

Randomize order across qualified participants; do not reveal all three simultaneously at first.

- $49 introductory access: what existing expense or workflow would this replace? What must be true to keep it after one month?
- $89 founding Core: would you choose access now, keep the money, or spend it on the current tool stack? Why?
- $129 standard Core: what additional verified behavior would make this rational? Do not imply Your Edge exists before sufficient history.

Then ask the participant to allocate a fixed $129 monthly research budget among TSM, their current tools, and unspent cash. Record the allocation and the first product removed. A hypothetical allocation is price-selection evidence only, not payment intent.

## Commitment ladder

Record the strongest unsolicited or directly accepted next step: no follow-up; interview follow-up; product evaluation; replacement trial; payment intent; refundable deposit (only after readiness and authorization); paid membership; second-month renewal; uncompensated referral.

## Synthesis rules

- Count participants, not statements.
- Separate swing from intraday and existing spend bands.
- A compliment is not intent; a hypothetical price is not payment.
- Report negative cases and quiet-day rejection explicitly.
- Do not expose private trade/account data in the repository.
